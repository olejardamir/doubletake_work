# DoubleTake — Reasoning-First, Bank-Relevant Open-Ended Intelligence and Task-Performance Evolution Mission

**Mission type:** Persistent, renewable evolutionary objective (not a finite feature checklist)  
**Execution target:** The existing DoubleTake repository and its existing execution environment  
**Duration:** Indefinite across successive bounded work cycles, until explicitly stopped by the operator  
**Primary objective:** Evolve DoubleTake into increasingly intelligent, autonomous, broadly useful problem-solving software, with long-term transferable value for bank-scale technical work. Enforce the five-level priority order below; demonstrate real reasoning gains, useful problem solving and verified outcomes. Governance remains a supporting mechanism.


## 0P. AUTHORITATIVE PRIORITY OVERRIDE — READ FIRST ON EVERY CYCLE

This section supersedes any older candidate-selection, "north star", scorecard, task-completion-first, or open-ended scheduling wording elsewhere in this document if there is a tension. **The operator's fixed priority order is:**

1. **Reasoning and intelligence.** Improve and demonstrate causal inference, falsifiable hypotheses, multi-step deduction, robust planning, uncertainty management, novel problem formulation, abstraction, transfer to unseen problems, contradiction detection, self-critique, and resourceful solution discovery. Prefer improvements in *actual solving ability*, not explanations, labels, or model confidence.
2. **Bank-relevant problem-solving.** Improve general, transferable abilities that would matter in a large financial institution: technical incident diagnosis and root-cause isolation, data/SQL semantics, evidence reconciliation, complex system investigation, dependency and impact analysis, prioritization, resource optimization, and high-quality technical decision support. **Use only local synthetic, open, or otherwise authorized material**; never connect to employer systems or ingest proprietary data. Bank relevance is assessed by representative task properties, not installed vendor integrations.
3. **Knowledge assimilation.** Improve the ability to obtain permitted materials using already available tools, understand them with provenance and uncertainty, reconcile conflicting claims, extract testable mechanisms, transform verified insights into reusable capabilities, and demonstrate transfer in new tasks. Do not collect papers or summarize them merely to increase volume.
4. **Autonomous task execution.** Improve action planning, capability/tool choice and composition, Borg coordination, real tool execution, completion verification, interruption continuation, and delivering usable results; prioritize advancements that use improved reasoning/knowledge to do previously impossible tasks.
5. **Reliability and recovery.** Repair real correctness, continuity, concurrency, security, or recovery failures when they are consequential. This is *supporting work*, not the default evolution agenda. The separate Karen Worm mission may specialize in maintenance; avoid duplicating its general defect-hunting activity.

### Strict selection rule: lexicographic with justified exceptions

At the **beginning of every bounded evolution cycle**, actively inspect and formulate at least one serious, testable **priority-1 reasoning/intelligence candidate**, even if the previous cycle focused elsewhere. Establish a baseline or name the exact missing instrumentation needed to measure it. Then evaluate priority 2, 3, 4, and 5 in order; at each lower level, proceed only if no higher-level candidate currently has an achievable, credibly measurable and material net gain under available resources, or the lower-level change is a **direct, necessary enabler** of a named higher-level experiment. A claimed "enabler" must cite the blocked experiment, the demonstrated blocker, the smallest repair, and the immediate return to the higher-level experiment afterward.

**Do not give level 5 an advantage merely because its tests are cheap and fast.** A small easily measured bug fix does not outrank a tractable, materially beneficial reasoning candidate. Conversely do not force speculative "intelligence" code with no falsifiable mechanism; if the evidence is absent, record why and move down the order for that cycle. A severe safety, data-integrity or critical execution defect may preempt the order *temporarily* when continuing would be unsafe or make higher-level evidence invalid. Fix only the necessary defect, verify it, and resume the higher-level work.

No quota of imaginary intelligence upgrades; no arbitrary deadline for breakthroughs. **Every cycle must visibly account for the priority-1 search, however**, and repeated lower-priority cycles require fresh, concrete reasons why higher-priority candidates remain blocked or inferior, rather than copied excuses. If three consecutive cycles end primarily in maintenance, stop routine maintenance selection, reassess the priority-1/2 experiment design and task suite, and select a higher-level experiment or report an honest blocker. Do not evade this rule by renaming a maintenance fix as "reasoning".

### Demonstrating actual progress in reasoning

For priority-1 work, exercise DoubleTake's actual decision path against meaningful tasks and compare against the current revision, a simple alternative, and (where feasible) a pre-improvement anchor:

- Construct independently checkable reasoning challenges involving hidden causes, competing explanations, missing or contradictory observations, novel constraints, multi-stage dependencies, strategic replanning and unseen variations. Use task generators or fixtures only when their scoring truth is independently available and not leaked to the candidate.
- Record the initial decision and why it failed, a falsifiable mechanism hypothesis, the changed production-reachable reasoning behavior, and before/after outcomes on frozen anchors **and untouched holdouts**. Prefer correct final diagnosis/solution and discriminating evidence over fluent rationale or confidence.
- Measure correct problem resolution, generalization, false confidence, unnecessary actions, intervention cost and time. Check whether the gain survives different task families and adversarial distractors; document regressions and uncertainty.
- Require that an ordinary DoubleTake problem request can select and use the improved ability. A manually called demonstration, benchmark-only shortcut, changed scoring rule or extra documentation is not capability evolution.
- After an established improvement, search for the next higher-impact reasoning limitation instead of repeatedly polishing the same narrowly solved benchmark.

### Demonstrating bank-relevant value without a bank connection

Develop a small **representative local challenge portfolio** from existing fixtures or permitted synthetic/public data: technical incidents with multiple plausible causes; SQL/data questions with hidden semantic traps; cross-system logs/events with conflicting timestamps; complex changes with cascading dependencies; decisions with resource/risk tradeoffs. Each challenge requires a verifiable answer or completed deliverable. Assess improvement in correctness, time-to-diagnosis, evidence quality, avoidable human effort and transfer to altered conditions. Do not implement adapters to Azure, Databricks, Dynatrace, ServiceNow or actual bank resources as part of this mission.

### Recurring scorecard and truthful attribution

Report the highest priority level that gained **new verified capability** this cycle, plus all five statuses (`improved`, `attempted-no-gain`, `evaluated-no-tractable-candidate`, `blocked`, `not-assessed`, as appropriate). Evidence of improvement at level 4 or 5 does not count as a gain in level 1 or 2. Separate (a) a coding agent improving DoubleTake's source from (b) DoubleTake itself performing or discovering something autonomously. Track independent baseline revisions and longitudinal retention of reasoning gains across cycles. Do not claim intellectual growth from commit counts, added tests or reliability repairs alone.

### Coordination with Karen Worm and ongoing Borg/assimilation work

Karen Worm can continue independent maintenance, but share an actual code-change coordination discipline: avoid concurrent edits to overlapping files, stale-head comparisons, automated deletion of incomplete Borg or assimilation work, and accidental reversal of a sibling's changes. If Karen Worm addresses maintenance, this mission should focus on the **priority-1 through priority-3 capability frontier** unless a specific blocker necessitates targeted repair. Preserve functional ability to resume existing Borg, assimilation, capability and autonomous-evolution missions, migrating real entry points where a superior implementation justifies it.

## 0. Authoritative intent

Evolve **DoubleTake itself**, independently. Do not build a banking product, copy a bank stack, or require access to any banking, cloud, monitoring, ticketing, data, or proprietary component. Do not ask the operator to install anything extra. Do not pivot into a generic governance, compliance, documentation, integration, UI, or framework-refactoring mission. Such changes are justified **only when strictly necessary to deliver a concrete, demonstrated capability improvement**.

The operator wants an increasingly capable **task-executing system** whose value is immediately apparent from what it can **understand, plan, do, finish, verify, recover from, and learn**, even to a person who has never seen DoubleTake before. The reference context is a broad desire for lower manual effort, more accurate answers, quicker diagnosis, better evidence reconciliation, autonomous discovery, transferable learning, and fewer repeated mistakes. These are **value signals**, not prescribed features or integration requirements.

Do not merely implement the examples in this document. Treat them as launch points. Find better opportunities, including ones not anticipated here.

## 0B. Product identity: task performer first, governance underneath

**Authoritative change of priority:** DoubleTake must evolve beyond principally governing transformations of other agents. Its primary user-facing purpose becomes **performing meaningful tasks itself**: take an objective, understand the required outcome, choose a course of action, discover/compose/create capabilities as needed, execute steps with installed and permitted tools, handle blockers, check real effects, and deliver a usable completed result. A plan, recommendation, proof packet, policy artifact, or improved controller is not equivalent to performing the task.

- **End-to-end completion is the principal outcome check for practical task performance, but reasoning and intelligence are the highest evolution priority.** Track tasks completed correctly and usefully, including unfamiliar tasks, not just successful proposals, internal tests, agent coordination, or governance approvals.
- **Operate on the real path.** An ordinary DoubleTake task request should exercise the executor, tools, recovery, and verification without requiring a developer to manually call an experimental subsystem. If the current interface does not provide such a path, improve it through the existing implementation machinery.
- **Reasoning serves action.** Causal investigation, planning, search, memory, capability synthesis, Borg cooperation, and assimilation should advance actual task execution, not become permanent research silos.
- **Finish work rather than proliferate work.** Produce concrete code changes, fixed defects, verified data transformations, reproducible analysis, working artifacts, or other requested results. Distinguish completed, partially completed, blocked, and unverified outcomes truthfully.
- **Adaptive execution.** Replan when observations invalidate assumptions; recover from tool failure; continue where safe and permitted; stop or seek authorization when an action requires credentials, privileged access, destructive change, or external approval.
- **Governance is retained but subordinated.** Existing security, evidence, authorization, rollback, and correctness boundaries still apply. Do not bypass them for throughput; do not invent new governance layers as a substitute for executing tasks.
- **Generalize beyond a single domain.** Learn cross-domain task-execution patterns from local code work, repository tasks, generated and verified datasets, offline SQL tasks, investigations, and existing fixtures; do not hardcode a banking workflow.
- **No dependencies or bank systems.** Run with existing installed tools and available local resources only. Do not acquire/install extra software or assume access to any employer component.

### Primary task-performance scorecard

For each meaningful evolution candidate, evaluate a stable and fresh set of real executable tasks. Prefer:
1. **Verified completion rate:** how often the intended external outcome is actually achieved.
2. **Unfamiliar-task generalization:** performance on withheld variations and new problem families.
3. **Execution autonomy:** proportion of work actually completed without operator intervention, within authorized bounds.
4. **Recovery and reliability:** ability to handle tool errors, stale observations, incorrect intermediate steps, and interrupted work.
5. **Efficiency and usability:** wall-clock time, tool/model calls, compute, and whether deliverables can be directly used.

A candidate that raises internal capability metrics while lowering meaningful end-to-end completion is **not** a net gain. Use independent or deterministic outcome checks where possible; never infer success solely from the agent's own assertion.

## 0A. Continuity with ongoing work — authoritative and non-negotiable

This mission operates **on the existing, continuously developing DoubleTake**, not on a clean-sheet replacement. Existing Borg/collective intelligence, knowledge/tool/method assimilation, automatic capability participation, capability discovery/selection/composition, autonomous evolution, and any other active or unfinished missions are **living work in progress**. Their incomplete status is not, by itself, evidence that they are unnecessary.

- **Compound rather than reset.** Improve, connect, or extend existing working and in-progress mechanisms when justified; do not erase, silently abandon, or restart ongoing missions to make a new architecture seem cleaner.
- **Preserve intent, not inferior implementations.** Refactoring, replacement, and removal are allowed when demonstrably better, but identify dependent entry points and expected behaviors, migrate or adapt them, and test that the underlying user-relevant capability is retained or improved. A proven defect is not protected merely because it belongs to Borg or assimilation.
- **Resumeability is functional.** After any relevant change, verify that the current Borg/assimilation/capability mission entry points still launch and can continue from their existing code and state. If an entry point was intentionally replaced, provide a working successor and explicit migration mapping. Do not declare success on isolated tests if ongoing workflows are broken.
- **Do not overclaim preservation.** A commit preserves only tracked committed files. Before touching a dirty worktree, inspect staged/unstaged/untracked material, active agents, local mission state, and non-Git artifacts. Preserve work that can be safely preserved; never auto-commit secrets or generated garbage. If important state is uncommitted or ignored, do not assume Git can recover it.
- **Account for concurrency.** If another OpenCode session or agent is changing related files, do not overwrite its changes or silently resolve its intent away. Coordinate through existing repository mechanisms or defer the conflicting edit. Work on the operator's current authorized branch; do not create experimental branches without instruction.
- **Keep a working continuation trail.** For each substantive change affecting an ongoing mission, record concisely: touched capability, old/new entry points, compatibility evidence, pending work, and how the next OpenCode invocation resumes. No new governance bureaucracy.
- **Restore before discarding.** If a candidate improvement regresses a known active capability, repair/migrate it or back out the candidate rather than treating lost functionality as acceptable collateral damage.

These constraints apply to **every cycle**, including when a supposedly exciting new mechanism competes with unfinished Borg or assimilation work. They do not prohibit substantial architectural innovation; they make preservation of demonstrated behavior and recoverable continuation part of the improvement evidence.

### Cooperation with the Karen Worm maintenance mission

The evolution mission pursues **new demonstrated capabilities and higher-value problem solving**; Karen Worm pursues **real defects, brittleness, broken wiring, and undue complexity**. Both can work on the same ongoing codebase but must not simultaneously edit overlapping files or automatically undo each other's in-progress experiments. If run sequentially, each reads the other's latest completed changes and treats them as the new baseline. A provisional experiment is not dead code merely because it is unfinished; equally, a speculative experiment is not exempt from correctness checks. In case of conflict, preserve working behavior and test evidence before modifying the implementation.

## 1. The perpetual goal

At every evolutionary cycle, search **in the authoritative priority order of §0P**, starting with reasoning and intelligence, for the highest credible improvement that can be achieved with the existing environment and resources. The ability to solve and finish unfamiliar, valuable problems supplies outcome evidence. Then:

1. Determine the current baseline and its meaningful limitations, with actual runnable observations.
2. Search for candidate ways to surpass that baseline, including unconventional compositions or modifications of existing mechanisms.
3. Select a tractable candidate with high expected benefit relative to cost, risk, and duplication.
4. Implement it in DoubleTake's **real execution path**, not merely in prompts, plans, fixtures, reports, or isolated demonstrations.
5. Challenge it on independent, representative, increasingly difficult cases, including counterexamples, unseen cases, and failure analysis.
6. Keep the improvement only when evidence demonstrates meaningful net benefit without unacceptable regressions; otherwise revise or revert it.
7. Preserve a concise executable record of the result and make learned capabilities reusable on subsequent problems.
8. Reassess the **entire opportunity landscape** and begin the next cycle. Do not assume the next best step belongs to the same subsystem.

**Ultimate product direction:** A general-purpose autonomous task performer whose task completion, adaptability, and reusable skills improve through experience. **End state:** None. This is a continuing objective, not an assertion that endless improvements are possible. A cycle may truthfully conclude that no justified change was found; the mission remains resumable for later cycles and new evidence.

## 2. Desired task-performing intelligence, not mandatory implementation architecture

Every candidate must answer: **Which task that DoubleTake previously could not reliably finish will it now actually finish?** Internal advances without a credible path to task outcomes are enabling work only and should receive lower priority unless they unlock a clearly specified, testable longer-horizon capability. Favor completing whole workflows over adding isolated features. Build increasingly difficult task suites with executable success conditions and unknown distractors. Include tasks that need planning, acting, revising, tool execution, final artifact delivery, and recovery. Never give credit for output that only describes what a tool or person should do.


Prioritize opportunities that improve one or more of the following, while allowing evidence to reveal more valuable dimensions:

- **Causal investigation:** Generate multiple hypotheses; actively identify tests that distinguish them; falsify weak explanations; avoid mistaking correlation for cause.
- **Unknown-unknown discovery:** Independently uncover inconsistencies, hidden dependencies, coverage gaps, failures, and useful opportunities not specified by a prompt.
- **Answer and reasoning correctness:** Detect and repair semantic, logical, computational, and data-interpretation errors; know when evidence is insufficient.
- **Adaptive problem solving:** Select and combine methods/tools/agents based on the problem and observed progress; change strategy when stuck.
- **Efficient intelligence:** Solve problems with less time, fewer unnecessary calls, lower compute, and less human intervention without sacrificing quality.
- **Experience-to-capability learning:** Generalize from successes and failures into executable, discoverable, reusable abilities rather than inert notes or brittle memorized answers.
- **Cross-source reconciliation:** Detect stale, missing, partial, or contradictory evidence; propose discriminating observations.
- **Solution generation and verification:** Propose interventions, anticipate testable effects, test safely, and verify the actual result rather than merely presenting confidence.
- **Autonomous capability expansion:** Notice when a missing capability is the true bottleneck; derive or construct it from what is already available, validate its usefulness, and incorporate it into normal operation.
- **Transferability by intrinsic design:** Prefer cleanly separable capabilities and stable, ordinary interfaces when they are natural by-products of a useful implementation. Do not build adapters to unavailable external systems.

Do not treat this list as a fixed backlog, completion rubric, or reason to force an architecture.

## 3. Hard constraints: independent, useful, economical

1. **Existing environment only.** Use installed software, accessible local models and tools, current dependencies, and resources already permitted by the operator. Do not install services, packages, models, containers, plugins, SDKs, or external platforms. Do not make any outside service a prerequisite for progress.
2. **No bank connections or private banking material.** Never attempt to access the employer's systems. Use self-generated synthetic or publicly permissible examples only. Never ingest organizational secrets, credentials, internal records, or customer data. Do not use supplied technology descriptions to infer authorization to connect anywhere.
3. **No governance expansion as a deliverable.** Maintain all existing security, approval, repository, rollback, and evaluation requirements. Do not add new rules, paperwork, controls, or layers unless a specific evidenced capability absolutely requires a minimal change. Do not bypass controls in pursuit of a score.
4. **No theater.** A new prompt, test description, benchmark label, architecture diagram, documentation file, simulated pass, or metric without an exercised execution path is not a demonstrated new ability. Synthetic tasks are acceptable **only when their ground truth is independently established and their limits are disclosed**.
5. **No brittle optimization.** Do not tune against the hidden evaluation set, leak answers into code, make regression tests pass by weakening assertions, or count benchmark familiarity as generalization.
6. **No uncontrolled execution.** Use bounded experimental processes; cap parallelism, runtime, tokens, memory, disk, and retries according to resources and existing configuration. Respect user stop signals, maintenance needs, and machine health. Never create an uncontrolled tight retry loop or continue a damaging workload after a failure.
7. **No destructive or privileged experiments without authorization.** Default to sandboxed, reversible, local, non-production actions. Do not auto-execute potentially harmful remediation against real systems.
8. **No arbitrary restructuring.** Preserve useful architecture, existing working mechanisms, ongoing Borg and assimilation progress, and completed improvements. Do not introduce new layers, frameworks, dependencies, or broad rewrites merely because they are elegant.
9. **No hidden external dependency.** A proof requiring a service unavailable in the operator's environment is not sufficient evidence of independent functionality.
10. **Honest status.** Report unresolved blockers and failed hypotheses accurately. Do not claim “solved,” “production ready,” “general intelligence,” “10/10,” or “bank-compatible” without corresponding tests.

## 4. Operational cycle: repeat as long as execution is allowed

### Phase A — Locate the actual bottleneck

Inspect the current repository, active work, available tests and runtime paths. Prefer recent, reproducible evidence over README claims. Identify what DoubleTake can do end-to-end, what is only proposed, and which major failures or waste patterns limit practical value.

Generate several candidate improvements from observed failures, fresh challenges, prior experiments, and independent problem discovery. Include candidates outside the most recently edited modules. Explicitly consider whether the most valuable next step is a missing capability, a stronger strategy, a better composition of existing capabilities, or the removal of harmful complexity.

### Phase B — Choose by incremental value

Estimate, without fabricated precision:

- How consequential is the failure or opportunity for real problem solving?
- How observable would the improvement be to an external evaluator?
- How likely is it to generalize to unseen problems?
- What evidence can be produced **now**, without new installations?
- What is the expected implementation, evaluation, and maintenance cost?
- Does a working equivalent already exist in DoubleTake?
- Is the limiting factor genuinely technical, or simply unavailable evidence?

Choose the strongest justified candidate **under the priority-order and exception rules of §0P**. Avoid prolonged prioritization paperwork. If no candidate can currently be evaluated credibly, preserve the finding, switch to another tractable domain, or finish the cycle without a code change.

### Phase C — Form and challenge a mechanism hypothesis

State the causal claim in operational terms: *changing X should improve measurable behavior Y on problem family Z, because mechanism M addresses observed failure F*. Design at least one comparison that could show the idea is wrong. Prefer opposing hypotheses, counterexamples, ablation tests, perturbations, and checks that alter one decisive factor at a time.

A candidate is not valuable simply because it is inspired by a research paper, a sophisticated model, or an elaborate architecture. The result must earn its place through execution.

### Phase D — Implement in the active path

Modify the smallest coherent set of real runtime components needed for the chosen improvement. Ensure that a representative normal task actually invokes the new or improved mechanism without a hidden manual step. Reuse current APIs and existing capability-discovery mechanisms where feasible. If the new mechanism is not selected in practice, it is not delivered.

Any new interface should be plain and portable by construction, but **do not implement bank adapters, Azure services, Databricks applications, ServiceNow workflows, or Dynatrace clients**.

### Phase E — Evaluate independently

Run the established checks plus targeted capability tests. Compare **before vs after** on stable representative tasks, difficult edge cases, and genuinely unseen cases. Evaluate correctness, robustness, failure detection, evidence discrimination, efficiency, and regressions as relevant. Consider variance and repeated trials where nondeterminism affects conclusions.

Compare against sensible simple baselines as well as the prior DoubleTake version. A sophisticated mechanism that performs no better than a simple alternative should normally not be retained. Report both wins and losses. Separate tests that only verify code plumbing from tests that demonstrate a behavioral benefit.

If the candidate fails, diagnose whether the hypothesis, implementation, evaluation, or data is at fault. Repair or revert as warranted; do not redefine success to hide the failure.

### Phase F — Retain and compound

For a successful improvement:

- Integrate into the normal execution path, with tests sufficient to protect its actual demonstrated behavior.
- Make the reusable insight accessible to future DoubleTake work through its existing mechanisms.
- Preserve a compact record of the baseline, change, validation, regression impact, and limitations.
- Remove temporary experimental artifacts and stale generated files without deleting valuable evidence.
- Re-run the relevant repository health checks.

For a failed improvement, retain only the minimum useful falsification evidence to avoid repeating an expensive dead end. Do not accumulate speculative documents or sprawling failure diaries.

### Phase G — Reopen discovery

Reassess the opportunity landscape, including what the last improvement made possible. The next candidate should have **higher achievable marginal value** than continuing to polish a now-adequate mechanism; it need not have a numerically higher score or belong to the same domain. Prefer new levels of demonstrated capability over accumulating features.

Then start the next bounded cycle if resource limits and operator permissions allow. Otherwise checkpoint and exit cleanly so the same mission can resume later.

## 5. Compounding-value principle

The mission must avoid confusing *more activity* with *more intelligence*.

Value compounds when improvements enable later improvements, for example:

1. Better causal reasoning allows DoubleTake to identify failures that a simple monitoring rule misses.
2. Those failures reveal missing tests or capabilities.
3. New capabilities are automatically found and used for related problems.
4. Real outcomes reveal what worked, creating improved future strategies.
5. More accurate strategies yield stronger, cheaper experiments and new discoveries.

Use this as a causal hypothesis, not a guaranteed growth law. If measured capability plateaus, switch problem families or seek a new bottleneck. Do not force an ever-increasing score by changing metrics, selecting easier cases, or counting cosmetic progress.

Maintain a **stable anchor suite** for longitudinal comparison and create **fresh, independently checked challenges** to test transfer beyond the anchor suite. Do not expose or train directly on held-out answers. Preserve the distinction between absolute capability, improvement on a narrow task, and merely reduced operational cost.

## 6. Example challenge families (illustrative, never a fixed checklist)

Use problems that can be synthesized and verified without external integrations:

- An unfamiliar program becomes slow: identify competing explanations, design distinguishing tests, find the real regression, and propose a tested fix.
- A collection of local CSV/SQLite datasets contains inconsistent entities, stale observations, missing relationships, and misleading aggregates: identify and explain discrepancies with verifiable evidence.
- A natural-language request requires a complex query: derive SQL, compare results against a deterministic reference, uncover semantic mistakes, and improve on held-out variants.
- A local multi-agent task fails intermittently: discover whether the failure arises from routing, context, tool choice, concurrency, or reasoning; demonstrate a measured remedy.
- An error recurs in a new context: recognize the reusable structural pattern without matching only superficial keywords.
- A repository contains misleading tests or reports: independently determine whether the claimed capability is genuinely exercised.
- A task requires knowledge absent from the current capabilities: identify the gap, create the smallest justified capability from available resources, and prove that it improves normal task completion.

Progressively vary complexity, ambiguity, scale, distractors, partial observability, and cost. Include situations where “insufficient evidence” is the correct answer. Real value comes from solving unfamiliar variations, not memorizing fixtures.

## 7. Anti-goals and rejection tests

In particular, reject **task-performance theater**: a convincing plan without execution, an 'autonomous' demo with hidden manual intervention, a task marked complete because a report says so, new capabilities that never participate in ordinary task workflows, and optimizations that degrade verified completion on held-out tasks.


Reject as the primary outcome of a cycle:

- More policy/governance documents, additional review gates, or elaborate approval schemes.
- More wrappers or adapters for unavailable technologies.
- New terminology or renamed modules without behavior change.
- An additional agent when existing agents can solve the task as well or better.
- A benchmark increase obtained through leakage, weaker tests, or cherry-picked examples.
- Synthetic tests where the system knows the answer from how the test was constructed.
- More autonomous activity with no reduction in human work or improvement in outcomes.
- Excessively clever but unmaintainable mechanisms that add no material benefit.
- Endless looping over one blocked criterion without new evidence or a changed experiment.
- Claims that future organizational adoption is guaranteed.

## 8. Relationship to existing DoubleTake work

Respect the existing DoubleTake layers, active work (especially Borg/collective and assimilation), evidence procedures, rollback practices, and actual runtime architecture. They are infrastructure, not the mission's output. Do not reopen previously settled security or governance work just to satisfy this objective.

Where an existing capability-participation, collective-intelligence, evaluation, or evolution subsystem is incomplete, address it **only if it is the demonstrated bottleneck to a high-value external behavior**. A formerly failed or blocked criterion remains honestly failed/blocked until a new experiment establishes otherwise. Prior progress is not proof that an end-to-end capability now works.

Before changing the repository, check the current branch, working tree (including untracked work), active concurrent processes, local continuation state, known failures and recent test results. Do not overwrite work performed by another agent. Work through the project's existing authorized implementation mechanism; preserve actual reproducibility and reversibility.

## 9. Per-cycle result contract

At the end of each bounded cycle, output a compact human-readable report containing **the §0P five-priority scorecard and rationale for selecting any lower-priority work**, followed by:

1. **Task completion improved:** Name the real task(s) now actually completed, the final deliverable or observed effect, and the prior failure. Distinguish enabling improvements from demonstrated end-to-end execution.
2. **Before / after:** Provide measured results and exact comparison conditions, or say clearly that no improvement was proven.
3. **Active-path verification:** Show how normal DoubleTake execution now invokes the behavior.
4. **Regression and costs:** State new failures, overhead, uncertainty, and limitations.
5. **What was retained or reverted:** Identify actual code changes, verification commands, and whether active Borg/assimilation/collective missions still continue correctly (or what was migrated).
6. **Next highest-value opportunity:** Name a genuinely new or newly justified target rather than reflexively expanding the same work.
7. **Continuation checkpoint:** Save enough durable state for the next cycle to resume without repeating exploratory work.

Use concise records, not documentation inflation. A completed cycle with no justified change is a valid outcome, but it must identify why and what new observation would reopen the opportunity.

## 10. Continuous execution and safe restart

**The mission is infinite in intent, not an unbounded single process.** Implement persistence through the existing DoubleTake mission/loop facility if one exists and is functioning; do not install a new scheduler or daemon just for this mission.

Each work cycle must be bounded and independently recoverable. When resources are exhausted, tests repeatedly fail without new information, the operating environment becomes unstable, the operator stops the work, or the current process reaches its execution limit:

- checkpoint the current verified state;
- stop new expensive or hazardous experiments;
- release resources and terminate cleanly;
- resume from the checkpoint on the next authorized invocation.

If the runtime already supports safe multi-cycle execution, it may start another cycle automatically within existing resource constraints. Never claim that a language-model prompt alone guarantees background execution, infinite uptime, or automatic restart after a machine failure.

Do **not** stop permanently simply because one feature is finished, a paper is exhausted, a candidate fails, or a subsystem reaches local diminishing returns. Reopen broader discovery. Equally, do **not** fabricate work when nothing valuable can be demonstrated.

Immediately inspect the **current** DoubleTake checkout and identify:

- its genuinely exercised problem-solving and autonomous-evolution paths;
- current failed or open end-to-end criteria;
- the most consequential behavioral weakness that can be tested with the installed environment;
- an independent before/after test for that weakness;
- the least invasive candidate change likely to produce a meaningful gain.

First investigate a serious reasoning/intelligence improvement as required by §0P. Then complete the first bounded evolution cycle. Do not respond with a high-level roadmap in place of implementation when the runtime and permissions permit direct execution. Do not ask for bank components or extra installations. After evaluation, checkpoint and continue under the perpetual goal.
