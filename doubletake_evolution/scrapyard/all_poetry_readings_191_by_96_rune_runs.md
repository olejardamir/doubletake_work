## 173. ᛘ — Mager / Madhr

**Tradition:** SWEDISH_STIERNHIELM_1685

**Original poem**

```text
Mager Mullägor; try Lius; id est gyllen ahr
```

**English literal rendering**

Meagre earth-fields; three lights; interpreted as a golden year.

**Interpretation**

Wealth draws people because it brings comfort, pleasure and room to act. It can secure a household, enlarge choice and make life easier. At the same time, wealth quickly becomes a source of rivalry when several people want the same thing or feel entitled to it. That is why strife so often appears beside possession.

Guarded wealth becomes dangerous in its own way. Treasure that is buried, hidden or defended too fiercely begins to control the one who keeps it. What should have served life turns into anxiety, secrecy and constant watchfulness. A hoard that never circulates becomes a burden instead of a support.

The real question is not how much has been gathered, but what the gathered wealth is doing. If it creates freedom, security and the ability to help, it is functioning well. If it creates envy, fear and hard defensive living, it has begun to own its owner.

Keep resources moving toward use, stability and honest exchange. Hold what truly supports life. Loosen what has turned into dead weight. Prosperity is strongest when possession and generosity can exist together without taking freedom away.

## 170. ᛏ — Tider / Tyri

**Tradition:** SWEDISH_STIERNHIELM_1685

**Original poem**

```text
Tider Wintrom ledast; Tyri brinner bäst; id est dyrast
```

**English literal rendering**

Times in winter are worst; resinous torchwood burns best; interpreted as costly/hard.

**Interpretation**

Fire makes the dark usable. A torch is warmth, visibility and the sudden return of orientation when things would otherwise remain hidden. Its light is immediate and practical. It lets life continue where cold and darkness would shut it down.

Brightness also attracts attention. What is lit can be seen, judged and approached. Illumination therefore carries both comfort and exposure. It relieves fear, yet it removes concealment.

A living flame depends on care. Left untended it dies, and left uncontrolled it harms. Useful fire stays within form. It gives what is needed without taking more than it should.

Keep a light alive where it truly serves. Bring warmth where life has become cold, and clarity where confusion has made movement difficult. Steady usefulness matters more than spectacle.

## 166. ᚾ — Nöd

**Tradition:** SWEDISH_STIERNHIELM_1685

**Original poem**

```text
Nöd enda kost; nordanwäder kallast; id est dyr Tijd
```

**English literal rendering**

Need is the only choice; north-weather is cold; interpreted as costly/hard times.

**Interpretation**

Need narrows life. Under pressure, many possible choices disappear and only the essential remains. Hardship strips away decoration and asks what absolutely must be done.

That narrowing is painful, but it is also clarifying. A person under true necessity can no longer pretend that every preference is equally real. Shelter, warmth, food, debt, obligation and immediate survival move to the front.

This is where discipline becomes more useful than wishful thinking. Spend energy on what prevents collapse. Name the binding problem honestly. Save refinement for later if later becomes possible.

Constraint should not be mistaken for final defeat. It is a severe teacher of priorities. Once the essential is protected, freedom can begin to return. Until then, realism is stronger than fantasy.

## 54. ᛁ — Íss

**Tradition:** ICELANDIC_NORMALIZED_REFERENCE

**Original poem**

```text
Íss er árbörkr
ok unnar þak
ok feigra manna fár.
glacies jöfurr.
```

**English literal rendering**

Ice is river-bark, the roof of the wave, and danger/destruction to doomed people. [Latin: ice.]

**Interpretation**

Ice creates beauty and danger at the same time. It shines, forms a path and gives shape to what would otherwise remain flowing, yet it also hides depth and makes every step uncertain.

A smooth surface can be very misleading. What looks stable may be only temporarily stable. What looks solid may rest over movement, cold and hidden risk. Slipping often comes from trusting appearance too quickly.

Temporary support still has value. A crossing may be possible. A structure may hold. The mistake lies in confusing use with permanence. Conditions that are adequate now may fail under different weight, heat or pressure.

Move with caution across beautiful certainties. Appreciate clarity, but do not let clarity become naivety. Safety grows from remembering what the shining surface covers.

At the same time, wealth quickly becomes a source of rivalry when several people want the same thing or feel entitled to it. That is why strife so often appears beside possession.

Guarded wealth becomes dangerous in its own way. Treasure that is buried, hidden or defended too fiercely begins to control the one who keeps it. What should have served life turns into anxiety, secrecy and constant watchfulness. A hoard that never circulates becomes a burden instead of a support.

The real question is not how much has been gathered, but what the gathered wealth is doing. If it creates freedom, security and the ability to help, it is functioning well. If it creates envy, fear and hard defensive living, it has begun to own its owner.

Keep resources moving toward use, stability and honest exchange. Hold what truly supports life. Loosen what has turned into dead weight. Prosperity is strongest when possession and generosity can exist together without taking freedom away.

Thurs/Thor enclosed or 'belg-bound'; a late calendar rune.

**Interpretation**

Harshness rules here. Torment, cliffs and giant-force describe something resistant, heavy and difficult to soften. This is not a mild inconvenience or a negotiable irritation. It is the kind of obstacle that stands in hard ground and makes its presence felt.

Distance, caution, preparation and a stronger boundary may be wiser than repeated attempts to win kindness from what is fundamentally rough.

The useful response is sober strength. Protect the vulnerable part first. Choose your ground carefully. Some conflicts are improved by confrontation, others by avoidance, and others by refusing to let a destructive force set the terms of life.

# Center
**Interpretation**

The center gathers the recurring conditions of life without collapsing them into one single image. Weather, growth, injury, work, travel, light, law, death, wealth, exchange and companionship all meet here. Life does not unfold in abstraction. It unfolds inside a world that feeds, pressures, limits and changes.

Human beings are never shown as detached from those conditions. They ride, labor, inherit, speak, suffer, heal, give, cross water, endure age and depend on seasonal change. Agency exists, but it is always situated. Freedom is real, yet it is always exercised inside some mixture of support and constraint.

The strongest balance in this center lies between use and excess. Wealth is good when it serves life and dangerous when it hardens into hoarding. Law is good when it makes shared life livable. Light is good when it reveals and warms. Rest is good when labor has spent the body honestly. Even joy appears not as empty excitement but as life relieved of unnecessary misery.

Another strong current is proportion. Not every obstacle can be broken. Not every season can be forced. Not every wound should simply be endured. Some situations call for movement, others for caution, others for repair, others for limits, and others for acceptance of larger conditions.

The soundest orientation is therefore direct and practical. Protect what is vulnerable. Use what truly supports life. Share what should not be hoarded. Move when the road is open. Yield when a larger condition cannot be bullied into changing. Keep clarity, generosity and proportion together. That is where a human life becomes strongest, most decent and most inhabitable.
