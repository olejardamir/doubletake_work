# 0. Governing instruction

Do **not** directly implement Family 116 as a software translation.

Do **not** reproduce its mathematical construction merely because it exists.

Do **not** create a subsystem named after the paper unless that is independently the best architecture.

Instead:

> Evolve DoubleTake from its current architecture until the useful mechanism-level knowledge represented by Family 116 is either surpassed, subsumed, or shown not to improve DoubleTake.

Family 116 is a source of **problem reformulation and mechanism inspiration**, not a specification.

The mission is to discover what deeper principle makes the source valuable, determine whether DoubleTake already contains part of that principle, invent superior DT-native mechanisms where possible, and retain only changes that demonstrate measurable value.

# 1. Source idea

Family 116 studies deterministic hitting points / hitting lists capable of exposing non-identity within bounded mathematical classes.

The transferable idea is broader:

> A system should not rely only on ordinary examples when deciding whether two mechanisms are meaningfully equivalent. It should actively construct compact, high-information probes designed to expose differences.

For DoubleTake, this suggests—but does not prescribe—mechanisms such as:

- discriminating probe generation;
- semantic counterexample search;
- compact adversarial case sets;
- boundary-directed test construction;
- witness-oriented verification;
- equivalence challenge generation;
- mutation-directed evaluation;
- invariant-derived probe synthesis;
- adaptive selection of cases with high expected discriminatory value;
- reusable probe libraries derived from prior failures;
- progressive refinement of verification sets;
- combinations of static analysis, symbolic reasoning, contracts, learned heuristics, search, and deterministic generation.

These are examples only.

DoubleTake must be free to discover mechanisms that are better than a literal hitting-set translation.

# 7A. Anti-local-optimum requirement

DoubleTake must not stop at the first mechanism that produces a positive result.

For every major retained mechanism, DT must generate and evaluate at least one **materially different challenger** before declaring that mechanism locally best.

A challenger is materially different only if it changes the underlying approach, not merely a parameter value. Examples include:

- deterministic construction vs search-based generation;
- invariant-derived probes vs mutation-directed probes;
- static structural analysis vs adaptive historical selection;
- symbolic or constraint-based generation vs heuristic generation;
- single-strategy generation vs a portfolio/scheduler approach.

The incumbent candidate and challenger must be compared under equivalent budgets and evidence rules.

If the challenger wins on the declared objective without violating invariants, it replaces or is combined with the incumbent.

If neither dominates, preserve only the Pareto-useful mechanisms whose distinct value is demonstrated.

Do not keep multiple approaches merely because they are interesting.

Before final closure, DT must also attempt to synthesize at least one challenger **from the weaknesses of the current best mechanism itself**.

# 8. Core safety rule

A newly discovered discriminator may provide **negative evidence**, but it must not self-certify a candidate.

A concrete semantic divergence may be sufficient to reject or block a candidate when it violates an existing governed claim.

However:

> failure to find a divergence is not proof of equivalence unless an independent proof mechanism establishes that equivalence over a clearly bounded domain.

The new mechanism must not silently turn heuristic coverage into formal proof.

Use explicit evidence classes such as:

- witnessed divergence;
- no divergence observed under bounded probes;
- independently proven bounded equivalence;
- inconclusive;
- unsupported;
- budget exhausted.

Use repository-native vocabulary if suitable equivalents already exist.

# 11A. Minimum value threshold for retention

A mechanism must not be retained merely because it is directionally better.

Before evaluation, define a **promotion threshold** appropriate to the measured domain.

At least one of the following must be demonstrated against the strongest existing baseline under equivalent conditions:

1. **Detection improvement:** at least a 10% relative increase in meaningful defect detection or mutation kill rate; or
2. **Efficiency improvement:** at least a 20% reduction in median cost or time-to-counterexample while preserving detection quality within 2 percentage points; or
3. **Unique-value improvement:** detection of a meaningful seeded defect class that the baseline repeatedly misses, with replayable evidence and no material regression elsewhere; or
4. **Pareto improvement:** statistically and operationally credible improvement in one important dimension with no material degradation in the others.

These are default floors, not targets to game.

If the measurement domain is too small or noisy for these exact numbers to be meaningful, DT must predeclare an alternative threshold and justify it before observing final results.

A mechanism that does not clear a predefined threshold must be rejected, reverted, or confined to experimental status.

Do not lower a threshold after seeing results.

# 14. Promotion criteria

A new mechanism may be retained only if all applicable criteria hold:

1. **Incremental detection value**  
   It exposes meaningful defects not already reliably exposed by the existing baseline, or exposes them materially faster/cheaper.

2. **No semantic weakening**  
   It does not weaken existing verification, governance, or security.

3. **Bounded cost**  
   Cost is controlled and justified by value.

4. **Replayability**  
   Divergence evidence can be replayed.

5. **No authority expansion**  
   The mechanism cannot promote its own findings into code without existing DT governance.

6. **No false proof**  
   “No divergence found” is never mislabeled as proof.

7. **Measurable superiority**  
   The retained design must demonstrate value under controlled comparison.

8. **Architectural fit**  
   It reuses or extends the correct canonical owners rather than creating unnecessary parallel infrastructure.

If a mechanism fails, remove or quarantine it.

The system must return a bounded/inconclusive result rather than continue indefinitely.

### 18.7 Force an equivalent candidate

### 18.8 Surviving mutation

A mutation that survives the current discriminator must remain evidence of a verification gap, not be silently treated as acceptable.

## 24.1 Adversarial obsolescence tournament

Before closure, actively attempt to defeat the current best mechanism.

Run a final challenger tournament containing at least:

1. the current best evolved mechanism;
2. the original baseline;
3. the strongest rejected alternative worth retesting;
4. at least one newly synthesized mechanism derived from Family 116 again from first principles;
5. at least one newly synthesized mechanism derived from the observed weaknesses of the current winner;
6. a deliberately simple baseline such as random or naive boundary selection to detect benchmark pathology.

Use equivalent budgets and frozen evaluation rules.

The final mechanism must remain non-dominated under the mission's promotion criteria.

If any challenger produces a credible improvement that clears the predefined value threshold, the mission is **not complete**. Return to the evolutionary loop.

## 24.2 Source-obsolescence proof obligation

Closure requires evidence for all three statements:

- **Coverage:** every materially applicable Family 116 mechanism identified during the mission is already represented, surpassed, or experimentally rejected.
- **Superiority:** the retained DT mechanism performs better than a literal or straightforward source-inspired translation on the relevant DT objective, or there is evidence that such a translation adds no value.
- **Residual search:** a fresh final pass over the source and its abstractions fails to produce a challenger that clears the predefined improvement threshold.

Only then may the source be described as **obsolete as a source of additional demonstrable value for the current DoubleTake architecture**.

This is a scoped engineering conclusion, not a claim that the source is mathematically obsolete or universally inferior.
