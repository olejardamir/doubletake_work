## Precise mechanism and limitation

DART distinguishes a **mechanically legal checkpoint restore** from a **semantically admissible restore**. Its reviewed recovery contract identifies a concrete failed subtask instance (including re-entry ordinal), certified commit/exit boundary, scope of replay, and permitted effects. A conservative producer–consumer relation is derived from declared read/write sets. If a committed downstream consumer depends on a producer's output, or an irreversible effect would be crossed, a local rollback is vetoed unless a valid compensation/joint rollback is already authorized. If no admissible checkpoint exists, the paper falls back conservatively rather than silently dropping committed downstream work.

Paper's Table 3 reports 35 admitted and 12 blocked events in its frozen five-domain calibration, with **0/35 audited unsafe admissions** and **0/12 audited false blocks**. These denominators are small and author-defined; the 54 comparable semantic rows are a different denominator, not 54 independent safety trials. Boundary configurations and effect policies were manually reviewed; no universal boundary inference, silent-failure detection or arbitrary irreversible-effect recovery is claimed. Its authors' general whole-task fallback must **not** be interpreted as permission to rerun real external effects.
