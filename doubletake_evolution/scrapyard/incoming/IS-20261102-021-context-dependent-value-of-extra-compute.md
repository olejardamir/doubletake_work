## Direct evidence versus transfer inference

The paper reports that the **sign** of the association between an uncertainty signal and the benefit of invoking an additional rollout/search optimizer can reverse across task environments and model backbones. Examples from its Table 1: FEVER entropy–utility Spearman correlation is -0.156 on Phi-3.5 and +0.428 on Llama-3.1; on Qwen3-4B, HotpotQA is -0.327 while APPS is +0.317. These are **author-reported correlations**, not causal DT results. In its Plancraft Qwen3-4B comparison, always invoking the optimizer worsened success from 29.8% to 22.8%; the proposed learned gate yielded 23.3%, still below the base-only 29.8%. The paper itself notes FEVER as a weak setting and that one fixed-direction baseline can match or slightly exceed its frontier there. Training needs exploratory episodes; reported runtime-token cost comparisons **exclude** this initial exploration cost (50 episodes per environment/backbone). The full reported project used substantial GPU resources; small DT evaluation must not assume the same effect or economics.

**Transfer inference, not an observed DT defect:** When a worker is uncertain because *evidence is missing*, more self-generated reasoning may compound an unsupported guess; when uncertainty reflects *several viable choices with sufficient evidence*, a bounded comparison may help. A universal rule such as "uncertain => spend more compute" could be harmful in the former class. This differs from simply prioritizing which capability to improve (IS-016) or proving a patch works (IS-020).
