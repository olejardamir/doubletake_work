## Mechanism, limitation and negative counterexample

A acquires epoch 41, prepares update, pauses; lease expires. B gets epoch 42, commits acknowledged update; A resumes and sends delayed 41 write. A destination that atomically rejects stale 41 preserves B's newer state; a lock checked only at task start does not. **Nuance:** highest-*seen* epoch is sufficient to reject 41 *after* the destination has processed 42, but may accept 41 **after grant of 42 yet before any 42 write**. Strict exclusion at grant requires authoritative current-epoch validation at the write destination or a synchronized handoff; do not conflate these guarantees. Fencing does not itself ensure acknowledged-event durability, flush/restart recovery, idempotency, valid owner authorization or lossless merge.

**Potential DT difference:** Public 3.5.2/B4 documents an isolated two-writer whole-snapshot acknowledged-loss characterization and no authorized canonical no-loss writer contract. Stale former-owner lease turnover is a **distinct hypothetical** failure mode, not established DT behavior. No current private DT lease/epoch contract, writer implementation, baseline stale-writer failure or safe owner-authorized fixture has been verified; a single writer or existing CAS could make this inapplicable. Related IS-010 history oracle, IS-005 invariant-confluence and IS-023 snapshot negatives are not substitutes for this exact lease-turnover case.
