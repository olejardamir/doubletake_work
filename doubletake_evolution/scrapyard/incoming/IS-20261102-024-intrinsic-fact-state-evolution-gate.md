## Primary source, what was examined, and reproducibility

- **Directly read:** Sebastian-Antonio Toma, Jean de Kernier, Mike Ply, and Oliviu Matei, *Beyond Downstream QA: Rethinking How We Evaluate Agent Memory*, arXiv:2610.22209v1, submitted **2026-10-15**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22209 (sections 1, 3, 6–7 and trajectory schema). Primary author claims: FEB-30 has **30 fact-evolution trajectories** in six diagnostic categories, with known atomic claims and expected create/update/reinforce/conflict/supersede transitions after each document; a representative memory system showed mean **16-percentage-point intrinsic/extrinsic divergence**, **44%** divergent trajectories under the authors' criterion, and **20%** correct operation classification. These are **author-reported on one evaluated system**, not independently reproduced, and **not** DT measurements.
- **Method read:** compare **actual stored claims after each ingestion step** to a frozen, independently defined expected fact state, separately from downstream question-answering. A correct answer can mask a wrong store; a wrong answer can result from retrieval rather than storage. Categories include false-positive compensation, retrieval bottleneck, classification, synthesis, temporal evolution, and multi-author attribution.
- **Limitations from paper §7:** generated artifacts may not generalize to real documents; 30 trajectories limit fine-grained statistical comparisons; similarity thresholds/LLM judges introduce noise; does not evaluate procedural or episodic memory. Both intrinsic and extrinsic checks are required.
- **Code/data availability not verified:** paper says benchmark and scorer were released; secondary indexing points to https://github.com/amazon-science/feb-30-benchmark , but an attempted public read returned **404 on 2026-11-02**, and a GitHub repository lookup found no match. This does **not** establish permanent absence or invalidate the paper. No code/data was downloaded, executed, or copied; code licensing unverified.
