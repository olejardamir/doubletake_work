## Directly inspected source evidence versus inference
Read the primary paper's arXiv HTML sections 3–6, Table 1, Appendix A and the meta-skill results in Table 11 (not just its abstract). The authors separate **experience generation**, **skill extraction**, and **actual consumption**. Their operational measure is held-out task performance delta for an extractor E, consumer M and domain D: performance of M with the generated skill minus M without it. They report five domains, six target models, five extractors, three evaluation repeats, and **negative transfer in 25% of the extractor/consumer/domain entries**; this is their experimental result, not a DT estimate. Some target models deteriorate with a skill that helps another. A generic prose-plausibility rubric reduced average performance by 0.59 percentage points in the nine tested guidance cells, whereas three empirically screened criteria (failure-mechanism encoding, actionable specificity, high-risk-action blacklist) improved the nine cells by 1.55 points on average. These numbers are authors' reported results, not independently replicated here.

**Important limitations:** arXiv preprint; no independent reproduction established in this run. Skills were consolidated to one domain-level artifact and injected into the system prompt, not discovered through a production retrieval/approval pipeline; no claim of DT transfer follows. Authors explicitly leave large libraries, selection, composition and richer harnesses for future work. The rubric evaluation uses prior high-gap skill-pair analysis, so treat its predictive/generalization claim cautiously until held-out validation. Never turn source prose into privileged instructions or import its examples unreviewed.
