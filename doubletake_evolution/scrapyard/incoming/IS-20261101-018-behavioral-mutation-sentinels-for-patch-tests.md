## Primary provenance and what was examined
1. **Directly read:** René Just, Darioush Jalali, Laura Inozemtseva, Michael D. Ernst, Reid Holmes, Gordon Fraser, *Are Mutants a Valid Substitute for Real Faults in Software Testing?* FSE 2014, DOI **10.1145/2635868.2635929**. Author-hosted complete paper: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/mutation-effectiveness-fse2014.pdf ; author bibliographic page: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/mutation-effectiveness-fse2014-abstract.html . Examined methods, empirical results and stated limitations. Authors studied **357 isolated real faults** across five Java projects, **~230,000 generated mutants**, and test effectiveness while controlling for statement coverage. Their study found mutant detection correlated with real-fault detection beyond coverage, but also found **17% of real faults uncoupled from their mutants** and another **10% requiring stronger/new operators**. These are authors' measurements, **not** Python or DT replication.
2. **Directly read abstract/limitations:** Petrovic, Ivankovic, Kurtz, Ammann, Just, *An Industrial Application of Mutation Testing: Lessons, Challenges, and Research Directions*, Mutation 2018. Author/institution page: https://research.google/pubs/an-industrial-application-of-mutation-testing-lessons-challenges-and-research-directions/ . Mutation adequacy at scale can be expensive or undesirable; many mutants are redundant/equivalent/uninteresting. Do **not** maximize a raw mutation score indiscriminately.
3. **Directly read abstract, introduction and method:** Ahmed et al., *A new perspective on the competent programmer hypothesis through the reproduction of real faults with repeated mutations*, 2024, DOI **10.1002/stvr.1874**, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/stvr.1874 . Authors studied 835 reproducible real-world faults in 17 Java projects and found common operators miss important fault structures. This qualifies the 2014 result; neither study proves an arbitrary synthetic mutation represents a real DT defect. No third-party code or full text was copied.
