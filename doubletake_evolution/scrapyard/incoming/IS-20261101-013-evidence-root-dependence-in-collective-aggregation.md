## Distinct mechanism and negative evidence

**Source fact:** Reports can be different in wording, author or model instance while descending from one underlying evidence root. Conversely, genuinely independent sources can produce nearly identical reports. A content-only aggregator cannot universally tell cloned from independent corroboration; the paper supplies a simple two-report counterexample. A second report from the *same* source is not always worthless: separate extractions may reveal additional aspects of that source, but their total information saturates. Shared base-model extraction errors can introduce additional dependence even across multiple generated reports. Thus neither report count, agent count, semantic similarity nor blanket one-root-one-vote is a valid universal evidence weight.

**Author-reported measurement:** Holding one evidence root fixed while increasing reports from 1 to 32 reduced an independence-assuming method's nominal 95% interval coverage from 0.940 to 0.263; increasing independent roots at fixed report count removed the failure at the independent extreme. The author's out-of-sample calibrated shared-extraction correlation estimate was 0.719. These numbers come from **synthetic evidence worlds and the studied model**, not DT or general deployment guarantees. The correlated-error correction is classified *exploratory* in the author's REPRODUCIBILITY.md; the Grid A/B design is described as confirmatory.

**Proposed transfer (unverified):** A Director may receive three separately worded reports from Internet Scout, Scrapyard Scout and a local DT researcher, but all three may derive from one paper, a single upstream synthetic test or each other's summary. Treating them as three independent confirmations could overstate the confidence of a Borg capability, acceptance gate, or assimilation proposal. The relevant distinction is **evidence-root ancestry versus report/agent multiplicity**. In public planning, record source-root ID(s), original/derived relation, independent reproduction flag and evidence owner before making confidence claims. Preserve unique extracts from a common root without counting them as independent experiments.
