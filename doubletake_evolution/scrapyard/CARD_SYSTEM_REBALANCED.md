Each card contains two states: the **primary state** and its **centroid-reflected mirror state**. Mirroring is not the same operation as Direct Opposite.

## Recovered geometry and tuning rules

The historical methodology defines the unmirrored deck as a **13 × 4 two-dimensional semantic gradient**. The rank axis runs from **Change** toward **Stagnation**; the suit axis runs from **Energy** through **Reorganization** and **Stability** toward **Inertia**. The mirrored deck was generated by representing the original words in vector space, finding a centroid, reflecting each vector through that centroid, and selecting the nearest suitable noun. Consequently, a mirrored word is not required to be a dictionary antonym of its primary word.

### Primary grid axes

- Rank gradient: Change → Alteration → Modification → Adjustment → Variation → Development → Progress → Evolution → Shift → Stasis → Inertia → Standstill → Stagnation
- Suit gradient: **♥ Energy → ♦ Reorganization → ♠ Stability → ♣ Inertia**

### Historical mirrored grid axes

- Rank gradient: Limitlessness → Adaptability → Versatility → Resurgence → Counteractive → Tautness → Doldrums → Fragility → Fallenness → Downspin → Formlessness → Unknowability → Lethargy
- Suit gradient: **♥ Disruption → ♦ Restoration → ♠ Durability → ♣ Consistency**
- Broader reflected tendency: **Reversal → Constancy**.

### Full-circle topology

The circular reading order follows the card sequence already built into the document rather than inventing a new traversal of the 13 × 4 grid. The **primary arc** runs through every unmirrored state from A♥ to K♣. At K♣ it crosses the same-card mirror edge from **Stagnation** to **Magnification**. The **mirrored return arc** then follows the historical mirrored states in reverse card order until A♥ **Reactance**, where the second same-card mirror edge closes the circle back to A♥ **Transformation**.

The two-dimensional grid remains the geometric regularizer for each individual state. The circular arc is a second constraint: consecutive states should form an interpretable semantic progression even when a suit boundary wraps from K of one suit to A of the next. ### Centroid-reflection rule

For one frozen embedding space, let the primary-state vector be `p_i` and let `c` be the centroid used for the historical mirror construction. The ideal reflected vector is:

`m_i* = 2c - p_i`

Equivalently, after centering on `c`, the mirrored direction is the negative of the primary direction:

`m_i* - c = -(p_i - c)`

This gives a useful continuity constraint. If two primary states form a local step `p_j - p_i`, then the ideal mirrored step has the same length and the opposite direction: `m_j* - m_i* = -(p_j - p_i)`. The mirrored surface should therefore preserve local structure rather than behave like an unrelated second vocabulary.

The exact historical embedding vectors and model identities were not present in the recovered introduction, so the historical mirrored nouns are retained as anchors. The present tuning changes definitions and relational structure around those anchors unless multiple independent embedding checks provide strong evidence that a noun itself should be replaced.

### Embedding QA protocol used for this revision

The revision was checked in three independent frozen local representation spaces: word-level TF-IDF with latent semantic analysis, character n-gram TF-IDF with latent semantic analysis, and a locally trained FastText representation. Raw coordinates from these incompatible spaces were **not averaged together**. Each space was evaluated separately and the evidence was compared through task-level quantities such as local-neighbor margins, mirrored-pair retrieval ranks, and centroid-reflection residuals. This is an audit of geometric consistency, not a claim that these local encoders reproduce the exact historical models.

### Relationship operators

- **Arc neighbor:** the preceding or following state on the full 104-state circle. Arc adjacency is a semantic-progression constraint and is not required to be an orthogonal grid move.
- **Same-card mirror:** centroid reflection from the primary surface to the historical mirrored surface while card rank and suit remain fixed.
- **Direct Opposite:** coordinate reflection across the center of the same 13 × 4 surface: `(row, column) → (12-row, 3-column)`. Thus A♥ ↔ K♣, 2♥ ↔ Q♣, and so forth.
- **Grid neighbor:** an orthogonally adjacent cell on one 13 × 4 surface. Grid adjacency constrains local coordinates; arc adjacency constrains the circular reading order. Both relationships are meaningful and neither replaces the other.

## Joker

The Joker represents the system-level interaction of structure, change, resistance, energy, regulation, and balance. It does not occupy a fixed cell in either 13 × 4 surface; it functions as an integrative lens over the geometry rather than as another point on the gradient.

## A♥

### Primary — Transformation

Transformation is a qualitative reorganization in which a system changes enough that its previous configuration no longer fully describes it. It involves more than adjustment or modification: relationships, priorities, or functions are reorganized into a new pattern. Successful transformation therefore combines substantial change with enough continuity for the new state to stabilize.

**Geometric position:** Within the original two-dimensional gradient, this state occupies the **Change × Energy** position. Its role is therefore constrained simultaneously by the rank-level tendency **Change** and the suit-level tendency **Energy**; neither axis by itself is sufficient to define the state. At this intersection, the rank contributes a strong tendency toward qualitative change, while the suit contributes active capacity to initiate or sustain change. The card meaning should preserve both contributions simultaneously.

**Foundation principles:** Transformation begins when accumulated differences can no longer be accommodated by small corrections within the existing configuration. The system must reorganize relationships or functions while preserving enough continuity for a coherent next state to emerge. Energy, force, and balance are used here as conceptual systems terms unless a physical system is specifically being discussed.



**Arc balance:** On the primary arc, **Transformation** is positioned between **Reactance** and **Innovation**. Its definition is therefore constrained not only by **Change × Energy**, but also by the requirement that it mediate an interpretable progression between those two neighboring states.

**Toward Reactance (circle-closing neighbor):** The circle closes across the A♥ mirror edge. **Transformation** and **Reactance** share card coordinates but occupy centroid-reflected semantic positions; this edge joins the two arcs without treating them as dictionary opposites.

**Toward Innovation (next arc neighbor):** The arc moves from **Transformation** toward **Innovation** while remaining in the **Energy** suit-axis and shifting the rank tendency from **Change** toward **Alteration**. The step is intended as a graded change in the same mode rather than a categorical jump.

**Mirroring toward Reactance:** **Transformation** mirrors **Reactance** at the same card coordinates. Mirroring is a centroid-reflection operation, not an antonym lookup: the pair represents corresponding positions on the primary and reflected semantic surfaces (**Change × Energy** ↔ **Limitlessness × Disruption**).

**Direct Opposite:** **K♣ — Stagnation**. Direct opposition is separate from mirroring: it reflects the card coordinates across the center of the same semantic surface.

### Mirrored — Reactance

Reactance is an active counter-response that intensifies when choice, autonomy, or available movement is perceived as being restricted. Within the centroid-reflected gradient, this state occupies the **Limitlessness × Disruption** position, corresponding to the same card coordinates as its primary-state mirror. Its role is therefore constrained simultaneously by the reflected rank tendency **Limitlessness** and the reflected suit tendency **Disruption**. The sometimes-unusual noun is retained because the historical system selected mirrored terms geometrically rather than by ordinary thesaurus opposition. At this reflected intersection, the rank contributes minimal imposed boundary and maximized openness of possible states, while the suit contributes interruption or breaking of established patterns. The historical mirrored noun is interpreted only in the sense that best preserves both contributions.

**Foundation principles:** The state must remain faithful to both reflected axes: **Limitlessness** determines its rank-level direction and **Disruption** determines the mode through which that direction is expressed. Accordingly, **Reactance** should be interpreted relationally rather than as a free-standing dictionary label. Its meaning is bounded by neighboring cells, its same-card primary mirror, and its direct opposite within the reflected grid.

**Centroid calibration:** The historical mirror pairing for this card is **Transformation ↔ Reactance**. The primary coordinate **Change × Energy** is reflected to **Limitlessness × Disruption**. Accordingly, **Reactance** is in balance only when its interpretation preserves both reflected tendencies; a reading that reduces it to a free-standing dictionary meaning, or treats it as a simple antonym of **Transformation**, falls outside the intended vector geometry.

**Toward Regression (previous return-arc neighbor):** The mirrored return arc moves from **Reactance** toward **Regression** while remaining in the reflected **Disruption** suit-axis and shifting the reflected rank tendency from **Limitlessness** toward **Adaptability**. This preserves the reversed local structure expected from centroid reflection.

**Toward Transformation (circle-closing neighbor):** The mirrored return arc reaches **Reactance** and crosses the A♥ mirror edge back to **Transformation**, closing the 104-state circle.

**Mirroring toward Transformation:** **Reactance** mirrors **Transformation** at the same card coordinates.
