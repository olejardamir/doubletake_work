# Historical Runic Poetry Corpus

## Purpose

This file collects the principal surviving rune-poem traditions and important textual witnesses as separate source traditions. It is designed so that each rune stanza can later be embedded independently without prematurely collapsing Anglo-Saxon, Norwegian, Icelandic, Swedish, Continental Germanic, or late-medieval material into a single modern "rune meaning."

### Conventions

- **Rune** = Unicode rune when a dependable Unicode equivalent exists.
- **ASCII** = convenient Latin transliteration / fallback identifier.
- **Original** = historical-language text or a conservative normalized transcription.
- **English** = a fresh literal rendering prepared for this corpus unless otherwise noted.
- Brackets `[ ... ]`, question marks, and labels such as `[uncertain]` preserve damaged, disputed, or uncertain readings rather than silently repairing them.
- A later rune with no surviving poem is recorded as **NO SURVIVING POEM STANZA**.
- The Elder Futhark itself has no surviving rune poem. The first 24 Anglo-Saxon runes descend from the Elder Futhark row, while the Scandinavian poems belong to the 16-rune Younger Futhark and therefore do not map one-to-one onto all 24 Elder Futhark runes.

# 1. Old English / Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem

**Tradition:** Anglo-Saxon / Old English  
**Alphabet:** Anglo-Saxon Futhorc  
**Poem coverage:** 29 runes  
**Textual situation:** The manuscript leaf is lost; the poem survives through an early modern transcription/printing.  
**Important:** Runes 25–29 are genuine Futhorc extensions beyond the inherited 24-rune row.

### 1. ᚠ — Feoh [F]

**Rune:** `ᚠ`  
**ASCII:** `F`  
**Name:** Feoh

**Original**

```text
Feoh byþ frofur fira gehwylcum;
sceal ðeah manna gehwylc miclun hyt dælan
gif he wile for drihtne domes hleotan.
```

**English — literal rendering**

Wealth is a comfort to everyone; yet each person should distribute it generously if he wishes to gain honour before the Lord.

### 2. ᚢ — Ur [U]

**Rune:** `ᚢ`  
**ASCII:** `U`  
**Name:** Ur

**Original**

```text
Ur byþ anmod ond oferhyrned,
felafrecne deor, feohteþ mid hornum
mære morstapa; þæt is modig wuht.
```

**English — literal rendering**

The aurochs is proud and great-horned, a very dangerous beast that fights with its horns; a famous moor-roamer, a bold creature.

### 3. ᚦ — Ðorn / Thorn [TH]

**Rune:** `ᚦ`  
**ASCII:** `TH`  
**Name:** Ðorn / Thorn

**Original**

```text

anfeng ys yfyl, ungemetum reþe
manna gehwelcum, ðe him mid resteð.
```

**English — literal rendering**

The thorn is exceedingly sharp; grasping it is bad for any warrior, brutally severe for anyone who rests among it.

### 4. ᚩ — Os [O]

**Rune:** `ᚩ`  
**ASCII:** `O`  
**Name:** Os

**Original**


**English — literal rendering**

The mouth is the source of every speech, a support of wisdom and comfort of the wise, and to every noble a blessing and hope.

**English — literal rendering**

# 3. Icelandic Rune-Poem Tradition — Normalized Comparative Reference

**Tradition:** Old Icelandic  
**Alphabet:** Younger Futhark  
**Coverage:** 16 runes  
**Form:** Three kennings/periphrases for each rune, commonly followed by a Latin gloss and an Old Norse ruler-word.

**Warning:** This normalized presentation is a comparative reference, **not a single definitive manuscript text**. The major manuscript and printed witnesses differ. Separate witnesses follow in Sections 4–8.

### 1. ᚠ — Fé [F]

**Rune:** `ᚠ`  
**ASCII:** `F`  
**Name:** Fé

**Original**

```text
Fé er frænda róg
ok flæðar viti
ok grafseiðs gata.
aurum fylkir.
```

**English — literal rendering**

Wealth is kinsmen's strife, the beacon/fire of flood or sea, and the path of the grave-serpent. [Latin: gold.]

### 2. ᚢ — Úr [U]

**Rune:** `ᚢ`  
**ASCII:** `U`  
**Name:** Úr

**Original**

```text
Úr er skýja grátr
ok skára þverrir
ok hirðis hatr.
umbre vísi.
```

**English — literal rendering**

Rain is the clouds' weeping, the destroyer of cut hay, and the shepherd's hatred. [Latin: rain.]

### 3. ᚦ — Þurs [TH]

**Rune:** `ᚦ`  
**ASCII:** `TH`  
**Name:** Þurs

**Original**

```text
Þurs er kvenna kvöl
ok kletta búi
ok varðrúnar verr.
Saturnus þengill.
```

**English — literal rendering**

The giant is women's torment, a dweller among cliffs, and husband of a giantess/ward-rune woman. [Saturn.]

### 4. ᚬ — Óss [O]

**Rune:** `ᚬ`  
**ASCII:** `O`  
**Name:** Óss

**Original**

```text
Óss er algingautr
ok ásgarðs jöfurr,
ok valhallar vísi.
Jupiter oddviti.
```

**English — literal rendering**

The god is aged Gautr/Odin, prince of Ásgarðr, and ruler of Valhöll. [Jupiter.]

### 5. ᚱ — Reið [R]

**Rune:** `ᚱ`  
**ASCII:** `R`  
**Name:** Reið

**Original**

```text

ok snúðig ferð
ok jórs erfiði.
iter ræsir.
```

**English — literal rendering**

Riding is the seated rider's joy, a swift journey, and the horse's toil. [Latin: journey.]

### 6. ᚴ — Kaun [K]

**Rune:** `ᚴ`  
**ASCII:** `K`  
**Name:** Kaun

**Original**


**Rune:** `ᚼ`  
**ASCII:** `H`  
**Name:** Hagall

**Original**


## 8.2 Jón Ólafsson of Grunnavík — *Runologia*, 1752

**Sigla:** JOa = Part 3, ch. 1; JOb = Part 3, ch. 3.  
These are large antiquarian lists of *dylgjur* / paraphrases. They often begin with the familiar three rune-poem kennings but continue with further alternatives. They are therefore **variant semantic inventories**, not one fixed 16-stanza poem.

The most consequential recorded variants include:

- **ᚠ Fé:** JOa includes `Fáfnis bani`; JOa also gives `þegna þræta`; JOb retains `grafseiðs gata`.
- **ᚢ Úr:** generally preserves the cloud / cut-hay / shepherd complex.
- **ᚦ Þurs:** generally preserves `kvenna kvöl`, `kletta búi`, `varðrúnar ver`.
- **ᚱ Reið:** `snúðig för` occurs in JOa/JOb.
- **ᚴ Kaun:** JOb has `bardaga för`.
- **ᚼ Hagall:** JOa gives `krapa-` in the second kenning and can substitute `skýja skot` or `silfr` for the third.
- **ᚾ Nauð:** JOa/JOb preserve `þungr kostr` and forms of `vosamlig`.
- **ᛁ Ís:** JOa has `unnar þekja`; JOa and related material give `feigs fár` rather than the `forráð` family.
- **ᛅ Ár:** JOb has `glatt` where other witnesses have `gott`; JOa/JOb include `algróinn akr`.
- **ᛋ Sól:** JOa/JOb use `hverfandi hvel` as a third kenning rather than A/B's `ísa aldrtregi`.
- **ᛏ Týr:** JOa explicitly has `sá einhendi ás`; later sets commonly use `Baldrs bróðir`.
- **ᛒ Bjarkan:** JOa gives `lítið lim`, `laufgaðr viðr`, `lundr fagr`; JOb gives `lítið lim`, `laufgat tré`, `vaxandi viðr`.
- **ᛘ Maðr:** retains the man / earth / ship complex.
- **ᛚ Lögr:** later forms vary between `glömmunga grund`, `grunnunga grund`, and related water/fish expressions.
- **ᛦ Ýr:** JOa includes `bendr bogi`, `fífu fleytir`, `fenju angur`, plus `bardaga gagn`; JOb has `tvíbentur bogi`, `bardaga gagn`, `fífu fárbauti`.

## 8.3 Olaus Verelius — 1675 selected variants

Verelius preserves scattered alternatives rather than one clean independent rune-poem recension. Keep these as separate semantic witnesses:

- **ᚠ Fé:** `Fáfnis beðr` — "Fáfnir's bed."
- **ᚢ Úr:** `versta veðr` — "worst weather."
- **ᛅ Ár:** `jarðar gróði` / `foldar fegurð` — "earth's growth" / "beauty of the land."
- **ᛋ Sól:** `hverfandi hvel` — "revolving wheel."
- **ᛒ Bjarkan:** `lunda fegurð` — "beauty of groves."
- **ᛚ Lögr:** `skipa fold`, `landa belli`, `hamra forron` — a distinct set of water-related circumlocutions.
- **ᛦ Ýr:** `bendur bogi` — "bent bow."

These should be embedded as **VERELIUS_1675** records rather than merged into a generic Icelandic stanza.

**Character:** A copy of the Norwegian rune-poem expanded from the standard 16 runes to 24 verses, adding *stungnar rúnir* (dotted/stung runes) and other medieval additions.

For the standard 16 base runes, use the **Old Norwegian Rune Poem** records in Section 2 as the base textual tradition. The distinctive value of AM 738 4to is the following eight additional verses. Several rune identifications and readings remain uncertain in the modern transcription.

## Addition 17 — [D] Stunginn Týr

**Rune:** `[D — stung Týr; Unicode form varies]`  
**ASCII:** `D`

```text
Stunginn Tyr særður ásinn djarfi
sá var í víga starfi
```

**English:** Stung Týr: the bold god was wounded; he was engaged in the work of battle.

## Addition 18 — [E] Stunginn ís

**Rune:** `[E — stung Ís; Unicode form varies]`  
**ASCII:** `E`

```text
Stunginn ís stendur reyndur
stuldur oft er leyndur
```

**English:** Stung ice stands tested/proven; theft is often hidden.

## Addition 19 — [G] Stungið kaun

**Rune:** `[G — stung Kaun; Unicode form varies]`  
**ASCII:** `G`

```text
Stunginn kaun stofnar mæði
stór sár trú ég blæði
```

**English:** Stung Kaun brings exhaustion; I believe great wounds bleed.

**Note:** *Kaun* is neuter, so a grammatically expected form would be *stungið kaun*; the manuscript/transcription has *stunginn*.

## Addition 20 — [P] Plástur

**Rune:** `[P — medieval p-rune; form varies]`  
**ASCII:** `P`

```text
Plástur mýkur mein sára
mörg er komin til ára
```

**English:** A bandage softens the pain of wounds; many have come to years/age.

## Addition 21 — [C/Z?] Knésól

**Rune:** `[C/Z? — identification uncertain]`  
**ASCII:** `C? / Z?`

```text
Knésól kallar reina
kemur í stríði skeina
```

**English:** Knésól ("knee-sun") calls/marks a horse or course [uncertain]; in battle it comes to scratch/harm.

## Addition 22 — [Æ] Æfingur / Æsingur?

**Rune:** `[Æ — reading/name uncertain]`  
**ASCII:** `AE`

```text
Æfingur öllum grómur
æpir barn kvelur dómur
```

**English:** The first line is textually obscure; the second says approximately: a child cries, judgment/doom torments.

**Note:** The transmitted name *Æfingur* may reflect a scribal error for a form related to *æsingur*.

## Addition 23 — [Ö] Tvíörvaður bogi

**Rune:** `[Ö — medieval ö-rune; manuscript form differs from the ordinary one]`  
**ASCII:** `OE / Ö`

```text
Tvíörvaður bogi úr stóðu ylgjar hvopti
örvar tvær á lopti
```

**English:** A two-arrowed bow [first line partly obscure]; two arrows are in the air.

## Addition 24 — [X?] Elli hvíld

**Rune:** `[X? — identification uncertain]`  
**ASCII:** `X?`

```text
Elli hvíld erfiði og elli
ei vill vera á felli
```

**English:** Rest of old age: labour and old age; it does not wish to be on a mountain/slope.

# 14. AM 746 4to — Separate Plástur Rune-Poem Stanza

**Rune:** `[P — medieval p-rune]`  
**ASCII:** `P`  
**Name:** *Plástur* ("bandage")

```text
Plástur er meina mýkt
manna græðing
og benja bót
```

**English:** A bandage is the softening of injury/pain, the healing of people, and the remedy of wounds.

**Textual note:** The middle phrase is supplied in the published discussion from another manuscript witness because the corresponding reading in the cited manuscript tradition is problematic. Keep this stanza separate from the AM 738 two-line Plástur verse.

# 16. Old Bø Church Rune-Kenning Puzzle

**Place:** Old Bø Church, Telemark  
**Date:** 12th century  
**Type:** Runic riddle/kenning inscription, not an alphabetic rune poem.  
**Importance:** The kennings correspond closely to rune-poem meanings and resolve to a personal name.

## Original runes

```text
ᛋᚢᛅᚠᚿᛒᛆᚿᛆᚱᛘᛂᚱ ᛬ ᛌᚮᛏᛂᚱᛒᚿᛆ
ᚠᛁᚮᚿᛌᚠᛁᚿᚴᛆᛏᛆ ᛬ ᚠᛁᛆᛚᛌᛁᛒᚢᛁ
ᚼᛂᛋᛏᛅᚱᚠᛆᚦᛁ ᛬ ᚯᚢᚴᚼᚢᚼᛁᛌᚼᚢᛁᛏᛁ ᛬
ᚦᚱᛚᛌᚢᚿᛌᛅᛚᛆ ᛬ ᚦᛏᛌᚴᛚᚢᚱᛆᚦᛆ
```

## Normalized text

```text
Svefn bannar mér, sótt er barna,
fjón svínkanda, fjalls íbúi,
hests ærfaði, auk høys víti,
þræls vansæla. Þat skulu ráða!
```

## English

Sleep is denied me by: children's sickness, the hatred of workers, the mountain-dweller, the horse's toil, the harm of hay, and the slave's misery. This must be interpreted.

## Kenning solution

| Clue | Rune | ASCII | Rune-name | Meaning used |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| children's sickness | ᚴ | K / G | Kaun | ulcer/wound |
| workers' hatred | ᚢ | U | Úr | bad rain/precipitation |
| mountain-dweller | ᚦ | TH | Þurs | giant |
| horse's toil | ᚱ | R | Reið | riding |
| harm of hay | ᚢ | U | Úr | rain |
| slave's misery | ᚿ | N | Nauð | need/distress |

The resulting rune sequence spells **GUÐRÚN / GUDRUN** by the phonetic values intended in the puzzle.

# 17. Runes and Signs Without Surviving Poem Stanzas

These belong in the symbol inventory, but **must not be given invented poetry**.

## Later Anglo-Saxon Futhorc signs beyond the 29-stanza poem

| Rune | ASCII | Name | Status |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| ᛢ | Q / CW | Cweorð | Present in later manuscript rune rows; no stanza in the Old English Rune Poem |
| ᛣ | K | Calc | Later Futhorc sign; no rune-poem stanza |
| ᛥ | ST | Stan | Later Futhorc sign; no rune-poem stanza |
| ᚸ | G | Gar | Later Futhorc sign; no rune-poem stanza |
| ᛤ | KK/K | Cealc | Very thinly attested doubled/variant Calc; outside the standard 33-rune row; no poem stanza |

## Late Icelandic/medieval names with no securely attested poem stanza in the material collected here

- `[D/Ð]` **dís / dí** — rare; sometimes a modified Týr-like rune.
- `[G]` **grafið kaun** — "graved/stung wound"; a rare alternative name for stung Kaun.
- `[Q]` **qvíði / kvíði** — "anxiety"; rare manuscript name.
- Some extended alphabet entries in AM 749 4to — **C, D, E, X, Z** — are named but have no periphrases in that witness.

These should be represented as `poem_status: none` rather than assigned modern divinatory meanings.

# 18. Source Notes

This corpus separates **historical source text** from **fresh English rendering** and preserves disagreements instead of normalizing them away.

Primary / public-domain text bases and scholarly witnesses consulted:

1. **Rune poems — Wikisource**  
   https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Rune_poems  
   Used for the Old Norwegian, normalized Icelandic, Old English, and *Abecedarium Nordmannicum* historical texts.

   https://heathengods.com/library/viking_society/The%20Icelandic%20Rune-Poem.pdf  
   Used to distinguish AM 687 d 4to (A), AM 461 12mo (B), AM 749 4to (C), Runólfur Jónsson (RJ), Björn Jónsson (BJ), Jón Ólafsson (JOa/JOb), and selected Verelius variants. Page explicitly cautions against treating a normalized composite as a definitive Icelandic text.

3. **Swedish rune-poem comparative material / Bureus and Stiernhielm**  
   H. F. Morland Simpson, “The Southesk and Other Rune Prime-Staves or Scandinavian Wooden Calendars,” *Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland* 25 (1891), especially the comparative table on pp. 270–271.  
   https://archaeologydataservice.ac.uk/catalogue/adsdata/arch-352-1/dissemination/pdf/vol_025/25_256_332.pdf

4. **Swedish rune-poem overview, Bureus and Granius transcriptions**  
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rune_poem  
   Used as a convenient cross-check for Unicode rune equivalents and the Granius 1600 material.

5. **Late additions to the runes / AM 738, AM 746, Lbs. 2334**  
   https://ordstirr.wordpress.com/runes/late-icelandic-rune-names/  
   Used for the late-medieval/early-modern additional rune verses. The site's editor explicitly labels several readings and translations tentative; this corpus retains those uncertainties.

6. **Old Bø Church rune-kenning puzzle**  
   Cross-checked through the rune-poem literature and the normalized inscription reproduced in modern reference material.

7. **Anglo-Saxon extended rune inventory**  
   The 29 poem runes are distinguished from later manuscript-row additions (Cweorð, Calc, Stan, Gar; with Cealc noted separately). No poem stanzas are invented for these later signs.

# Corpus Handling Rule for Embeddings

**Do not merge these records before embedding.**

Use a key of the general form:

```text
TRADITION__WITNESS__RUNE
```

Examples:

```text
OLD_ENGLISH__HICKES_WANLEY__FEOH
OLD_NORWEGIAN__STANDARD__FE
ICELANDIC__AM687D__F
ICELANDIC__AM461__FE
ICELANDIC__AM749__FE
ICELANDIC__RUNOLFUR_1651__FE
SWEDISH__BUREUS_1599__FA
SWEDISH__GRANIUS_1600__FA
SWEDISH__STIERNHIELM_1685__FA
MEDIEVAL__AM738__STUNG_TYR
```

Only after each witness/tradition has its own embedding should historical-lineage relationships be added as metadata or used for cross-space comparison.
