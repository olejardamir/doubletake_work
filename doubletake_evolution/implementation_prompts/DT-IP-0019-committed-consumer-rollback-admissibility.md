## Distinct proposed behavior
A checkpoint that is structurally restorable can be **semantically unsafe** after downstream consumers commit dependent results or irreversible effects occur. At a *real existing authorized rollback-decision seam*, consider a **read-only conservative admissibility discriminator**, not a new rollback controller: match exact failed instance and re-entry ordinal; validate certified checkpoint boundary, read/write dependency edges, committed consumer lineage, and effect/irreversibility policy. A dependent committed consumer, irreversible effect, wrong instance, missing edge, ambiguous provenance or absent contract **vetoes local rollback**. A vetoed checkpoint may be reconsidered **only** if a valid compensation or joint rollback is **already authorized under the owner contract**, including its dependencies and external-effect policy; otherwise retain the veto. This exception is not a grant of compensation authority, nor permission for whole-task replay. Such a veto does not itself prohibit other owner-authorized safe recovery; **whole-task replay is not automatically safe** with external effects. Never mutate protected history or infer that a synthetic file rollback establishes cross-consumer semantics. **Explicit prohibitions:** do not use speculative classification to re-seal, delete, quarantine, rewrite or otherwise alter retained history, or to waive historical-integrity refusal. For any external effect require the existing owner's explicit **idempotency, authorization, and no-loss** policy; do not replay or compensate effects by default.
