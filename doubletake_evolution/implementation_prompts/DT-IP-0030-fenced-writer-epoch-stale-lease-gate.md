## Distinct testable mechanism, not duplicated by existing prompts
**Hypothesis only:** If the real authorized B4/3.5.2 canonical writer uses expiring leases/epochs and delayed old-owner writes can reach a durable destination, a lock checked at task start or a separate read-before-write check can allow an old holder to overwrite a newer acknowledged write. A obtains epoch 41, pauses; lease expires; B obtains 42 and acknowledges a durable write; A resumes with 41. Require the **write destination** to atomically reject stale epochs at the mutation/commit boundary, or use immutable generation-specific outputs with an atomically guarded publication pointer. A separate prewrite check has a check-to-use race.

**Important contract distinction:** A destination tracking only the **highest epoch seen on writes** can reject A41 *after* processing B42, yet still accept A41 after the **grant of B42 but before its first write**. If the owner promises strict exclusion from grant time, require authoritative current-epoch validation at destination or synchronized epoch handoff. Do not falsely claim high-watermark fencing satisfies the stronger contract. Specify per-key or shared-snapshot epoch scope, monotonically increasing generation, atomicity, durability across restart, valid owner identity, token authenticity and fail-closed policy from the actual owner contract. Fencing alone does not prove acknowledged-event durability, flush/restart recovery, idempotency/deduplication, lossless merging, or authorization.

**Nonduplication:** DT-IP-0011 tests independent acknowledged-write history and no-loss; DT-IP-0012 tests conditional acknowledgement/CAS atomicity and replay; DT-IP-0006 addresses invariant-confluence; DT-IP-0019 handles committed-consumer rollback. None substitutes for the **lease-expiry, former-owner, destination-side epoch acceptance** and **grant-before-write** discrimination. Public B4/3.5.2's isolated two-writer snapshot-loss characterization is not evidence this stale-lease failure occurs. A single-writer architecture or existing atomic CAS may make this proposal inapplicable.
